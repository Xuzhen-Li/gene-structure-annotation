# Structural gene annotation: an overview

[中文](field-overview.zh.md) · [Knowledge units](README.md)

## 1. What it is

Structural gene annotation answers one question about an assembled genome: where are the genes, how are their exons and introns laid out, and which proteins and non-coding RNAs do they encode? The result is a set of gene models written as GFF3 rows, together with the predicted proteins (`proteins.faa`). What those proteins do (names, GO terms, pathways) is a separate layer, handled in [gene-function-annotation](https://github.com/Xuzhen-Li/gene-function-annotation). Annotating a eukaryotic genome is within reach of non-experts but remains a challenging task (claim 1). Plant genomes add to the difficulty because of their size, their many transposable elements and their variable ploidy (claim 2).

## 2. The questions and the concepts behind them

**Is the genome ready for gene finding?** Gene prediction depends on the assembly: an incomplete assembly can lead to errors in gene predictions (claim 3). The repeats should also be marked first. In **soft-masking**, repeat regions are written in lower-case letters and all other regions in upper-case letters; for some predictors this gives better results than replacing repeats with `N` (hard-masking) (claim 4). Which repeat library to use is the subject of [Soft-masking with a trusted TE library](trusted-softmask.md). Book: chapters 2 and 3.

**What evidence defines a gene model?** A gene model is built from the kinds of evidence listed in [Gene models and the evidence behind them](gene-model-concepts.md):

- *Transcripts.* Short-read RNA-seq and long reads can be assembled into transcripts; StringTie2 is reference-guided, and IsoQuant works both with and without a reference annotation (claim 5). Book: chapter 4.
- *Proteins from related species.* A protein-to-genome aligner can drive gene prediction when no transcriptome data are available for a genome (claim 6). Book: chapter 5.
- *Ab initio and learned predictors.* Deep-learning predictors infer genes from DNA sequence alone, but one such tool, Helixer, is described in its 2021 paper as outputting base-wise probabilities and not yet complete gene models, and another was trained on mammalian genomes (claims 7 and 8). Book: chapters 6 and 7.

No single line of evidence is enough. In a comparison on five plant genomes, transcripts derived from RNA-read alignments alone were not sufficient, and workflows that combine evidence-based and ab initio approaches were recommended (claim 9).

**How are conflicting evidence and earlier annotations reconciled?** *Combiners* merge several lines of evidence into one gene set. EVidenceModeler reports gene structures as a weighted consensus of all available evidence (claim 10), and TSEBRA chooses between the BRAKER1 (RNA-seq only) and BRAKER2 (protein only) predictions using their RNA-seq and protein support (claim 11). When a closely related genome is already well annotated, *lift-over* maps its genes onto the new assembly: Liftoff aligns the genes and keeps the structure of each exon, transcript and gene (claim 12), and LiftOn adds protein alignments so that annotation can be transferred between more distant species (claim 13). Book: chapters 8 and 9.

**What lies outside the protein-coding set?** Non-coding RNA genes and pseudogenes lie outside the protein-coding set; MAKER-P, an annotation engine extended for plants, added non-coding RNA annotation and pseudogene identification (claim 14). Book: chapter 10.

**Is the gene set good enough to release?** Completeness can be measured with universal single-copy orthologs (BUSCO) (claim 15). Completeness alone misses other errors: OMArk also tests the consistency of the gene repertoire against related species and reports likely contamination (claim 16), and PSAURON scores how likely each coding sequence is to be a genuine protein-coding region (claim 17). A 2023 comparison noted that robust standards to evaluate the quality and completeness of structural annotation are lacking (claim 9). A released file must also follow the GFF3 rules, for example a `Parent` link from exon to transcript to gene and a phase on every CDS row (claim 18). Thresholds and gates are written only in [`docs/EVALUATION.md`](../EVALUATION.md). Book: chapters 11 to 13.
